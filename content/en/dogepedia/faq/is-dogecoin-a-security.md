> **Not legal advice.** Laws differ by country and change over time. This page is educational background for shibes who see headlines claiming “Dogecoin is a security.” Talk to a qualified lawyer for your situation.

## Short answer

**Dogecoin is open-source peer-to-peer digital cash.** It launched in 2013 as a decentralized cryptocurrency: anyone can run software, mine (via the shared Scrypt AuxPoW merge-mine with Litecoin), send value, and build on top of the public network. There is no company IPO, no equity certificate, and no single issuer selling “shares of Dogecoin Inc.”

When regulators or lawsuits use the word *security*, they usually mean something closer to an **investment contract** in a particular business — not “any asset that can go up or down in price.”

## Why people get confused

Cryptocurrency news often blurs:

1. **The Dogecoin network** — the blockchain, open-source software, and global set of users/miners/nodes  
2. **Products built around DOGE** — exchanges, ETFs, brokers, apps, or companies that *use* Dogecoin branding or hold DOGE on your behalf  
3. **Marketing claims** by third parties — which may or may not match how the protocol actually works  

A listing, lawsuit, or product prospectus about a **company or fund** does not automatically redefine the open Dogecoin protocol itself.

## Useful framing (US-style “investment contract” intuition)

In the United States, courts often look at factors associated with the *Howey* test when asking whether something is an investment contract. At a high level, people ask questions like:

- Are you investing money **in a common enterprise**?
- Is there a **reasonable expectation of profits**?
- Do those profits come **primarily from the efforts of others** (promoters / a management team)?

How those factors apply is **fact-specific** and depends on *what is being offered* (a token sale by a company? shares in a fund? simply using p2p cash?). Dogecoin’s protocol was launched as a fork of an existing open-source coin for fun and tipping — not as a share in a corporation that promised managerial profits.

That historical and technical context is why many community explanations treat **DOGE-as-currency-on-a-public-network** differently from **equity or managed investment products**. Again: courts and agencies decide cases; this is not a verdict.

## What Dogecoin *is* (technically)

- **Decentralized ledger** anyone can verify ([What is a blockchain?](/dogepedia/articles/what-is-a-blockchain))
- **No CEO of the blockchain** — development is open and volunteer-driven ([Who owns Dogecoin?](/dogepedia/articles/who-owns-dogecoin), [Dogecoin developers](/dogepedia/articles/dogecoin-developers))
- **Utility as money**: send value quickly with low fees — tips, commerce, remittances ([Dogecoin has no utility?](/dogepedia/faq/dogecoin-has-no-utility), [Send and receive Dogecoin](/dogepedia/articles/send-and-receive-dogecoin))

## Practical tips for shibes

- Prefer **self-custody** wallets for coins you intend to keep ([How do I get a wallet?](/dogepedia/articles/how-do-i-get-a-wallet))
- Read the fine print on **exchanges, brokers, and funds** — those are separate products with their own rules
- Ignore anyone who DMs “we are the SEC / Foundation / Core, send DOGE to unlock your account”
- For compliance questions about *your* business accepting DOGE, talk to counsel in your jurisdiction ([How can my business accept Dogecoin?](/dogepedia/articles/how-can-my-business-accept-dogecoin))
